# Primary Questions
> How many 2–4 unit properties have you helped buyers purchase in the last 1–2 years?

> Specific number, locations, and examples.
{.is-success}

> Vague answers like "I've done plenty of investment properties."
{.is-danger}

---

> When you evaluate a multifamily listing for me, what numbers and documents would you want before recommending an offer?

> Rents, leases, taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance, vacancy, legal unit status, CO/rental registration, and actual expense records.
{.is-success}

> Only talks about purchase price, mortgage payment, and gross rent.
{.is-danger}

---

> How do you verify that all advertised units are legal?

> Checks municipal records, zoning, certificates of occupancy, rental registrations, and other official records.
{.is-success}

> Assumes the unit count is correct because the MLS, Zillow, or seller says so.
{.is-danger}

---

> What would make you tell me not to buy a property, even if I'm willing to make an offer?

> Gives specific examples such as illegal units, bad financials, zoning issues, severe deferred maintenance, or problematic tenant situations.
{.is-success}

> Acts like almost every property can be made to work or avoids giving examples of deals they would reject.
{.is-danger}

---

> How do you evaluate whether a multifamily property actually cash-flows?

> Accounts for taxes, insurance, utilities, vacancy, repairs, maintenance, CapEx, property management, and financing costs.
{.is-success}

> Treats cash flow as simply rent minus the mortgage payment.
{.is-danger}

---

> How familiar are you with the municipalities and counties I'm searching in? Are there areas where you'd tell me to use another agent because you don't know them well enough?

> Clearly explains where they have strong local experience and is willing to admit when another agent would be better for a particular area.
{.is-success}

> Claims to know every market equally well or dismisses the importance of local municipal knowledge.
{.is-danger}

---

> What are some multifamily deals you've advised clients to walk away from, and why?

> Gives specific examples and explains the financial, legal, structural, tenant, or zoning problems that caused them to recommend walking away.
{.is-success}

> Can't think of any examples or says they generally leave that decision entirely to the buyer.
{.is-danger}

---

> How quickly do you normally respond to texts or emails, especially when there's an active offer or inspection issue?

> Gives a concrete expectation, such as responding the same day or within a few hours during an active transaction.
{.is-success}

> Says they're "very responsive" without giving any actual expectation, or regularly takes days to respond.
{.is-danger}

---

> What buyer-representation agreement do you require me to sign? How long does it last, what geography does it cover, and how can I terminate it?

> Clearly explains the term, geographic scope, exclusivity, termination provisions, and any obligations you have after termination.
{.is-success}

> Pushes for a long-term, broad exclusive agreement, is vague about cancellation, or discourages you from reading or modifying the agreement.
{.is-danger}

---

> If the seller is offering less compensation than your agreement specifies, do I owe the difference?

> Gives a clear explanation of exactly when you could owe money out of pocket and how compensation will be handled before you make an offer.
{.is-success}

> Is vague, says "don't worry about it," or implies you'll never owe anything without explaining what the agreement actually says.
{.is-danger}


# Extra Questions

> How many of those buyers were owner-occupants who planned to rent out the other units?

> Gives a specific number and can describe several owner-occupied multifamily transactions.
> {.is-success}

> Has multifamily experience but little or no experience with buyers who actually lived in one of the units.
> {.is-danger}

---

> What documents would you typically request during due diligence on a tenant-occupied property?

> Mentions leases, rent rolls, rent-payment history, security deposits, utility bills, expense records, COs, rental registrations, inspection records, and other relevant municipal documents.
> {.is-success}

> Mainly relies on the listing information and seller representations.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How do you verify existing rents and whether those rents can actually continue after closing?

> Reviews leases, tenant status, payment history, security deposits, local rules, and any restrictions affecting rent increases or tenancy.
> {.is-success}

> Accepts the seller's rent roll at face value or assumes rents can simply be raised after closing.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How do you handle inherited tenants? What issues would you want me to investigate before closing?

> Looks at leases, deposits, payment history, arrears, month-to-month status, tenant disputes, notices, below-market rents, and applicable landlord-tenant rules.
> {.is-success}

> Treats inherited tenants as a minor issue or assumes they can easily be removed or repriced after closing.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How would you help me investigate zoning, nonconforming use, rental licenses, and certificates of occupancy?

> Knows how to check municipal records, identify potential problems, and involve the municipality, attorney, or other appropriate professional when necessary.
> {.is-success}

> Relies only on the MLS description or says zoning and CO issues are entirely the buyer's problem.
> {.is-danger}

---

> If a property is advertised as a triplex but municipal records show it as a duplex, what would you do?

> Treats it as a serious issue, investigates whether the third unit is legal or legally nonconforming, and advises against relying on its rent until the situation is verified.
> {.is-success}

> Says it's probably fine because the seller has been renting it that way.
> {.is-danger}

---

> If tenants are paying below-market rent, how would you help me determine whether I can realistically increase those rents?

> Reviews leases, tenant status, local rent laws, notice requirements, market comparables, and the practical likelihood of achieving higher rents.
> {.is-success}

> Says you can simply raise the rent to market after closing.
> {.is-danger}

---

> If the seller gives us a pro forma showing much higher rents than the current tenants pay, how would you treat those numbers?

> Treats projected rents as assumptions that need to be verified against actual leases, market rents, unit condition, turnover costs, and local regulations.
> {.is-success}

> Uses the seller's projected rents as though they are current income.
> {.is-danger}

---

> Do you have experience with properties where utilities are shared between units? What problems do you look for?

> Understands the implications of owner-paid heat, water, electric, shared meters, allocation issues, and the potential cost of separating utilities.
> {.is-success}

> Doesn't see shared utilities as financially significant or can't explain how they affect operating expenses.
> {.is-danger}

---

> What inspections would you recommend beyond a normal home inspection for an older multifamily property?

> Depending on the property, considers sewer scope, oil-tank sweep, lead, radon, chimney, structural, electrical, HVAC, roof, and other specialized inspections.
> {.is-success}

> Assumes a standard general inspection is always sufficient.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How do you approach negotiating a multifamily property after inspection or after discovering inaccurate financial information?

> Is comfortable requesting credits, price reductions, repairs, additional documentation, or walking away when the facts materially change the deal.
> {.is-success}

> Focuses primarily on keeping the transaction alive or discourages renegotiation after new problems are discovered.
> {.is-danger}

---

> Are you comfortable working with a buyer who does their own detailed deal analysis and may reject a lot of properties?

> Welcomes detailed analysis, understands that many properties may fail your criteria, and doesn't pressure you to make offers just to move the process along.
> {.is-success}

> Gets frustrated by detailed questions, thinks you're overanalyzing, or expects you to make offers quickly after seeing properties.
> {.is-danger}

---

> What are the most common mistakes you see first-time owner-occupant multifamily buyers make?

> Immediately gives several concrete examples involving legal unit count, tenant issues, expenses, financing, deferred maintenance, or unrealistic rent assumptions.
> {.is-success}

> Gives generic homebuyer advice that could apply equally to a single-family house.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How many buyers are you actively representing right now?

> Gives a straightforward answer and explains how they manage their workload and availability.
> {.is-success}

> Avoids answering, appears severely overloaded, or has no clear system for handling multiple active buyers.
> {.is-danger}

---

> Who covers for you if you're unavailable and a property I want to see gets listed?

> Has a clear backup arrangement with another agent or team member who can handle time-sensitive showings and issues.
> {.is-success}

> No one covers for them, meaning opportunities may simply have to wait until they're available.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How will you search for properties for me beyond simply setting up an MLS auto-email?

> Describes manually reviewing listings, monitoring new and coming-soon inventory, contacting listing agents, using brokerage networks, and looking for opportunities that fit your criteria.
> {.is-success}

> Their entire search process is an automated MLS email that you could effectively monitor yourself.
> {.is-danger}

---

> Do you have lenders you work with who regularly finance owner-occupied 2–4 unit properties?

> Can recommend lenders with relevant multifamily experience while making clear that you're free to use any lender you choose.
> {.is-success}

> Pushes one lender aggressively, receives unclear incentives, or only knows lenders focused on standard single-family purchases.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How do showings work for tenant-occupied multifamily properties? Are there usually notice requirements or restrictions?

> Understands that occupied units may require advance notice, coordinated access, and compliance with leases and local rules.
> {.is-success}

> Acts as though tenant-occupied multifamily showings work exactly like vacant single-family home showings.
> {.is-danger}

---

> When a listing says "investor only," "cash only," "as-is," or "buyer responsible for CO," what do those terms make you investigate?

> Explains that these phrases can signal financing issues, occupancy problems, municipal violations, deferred maintenance, legal-use questions, or CO responsibilities that need investigation.
> {.is-success}

> Treats the phrases as routine marketing language without asking why the seller included them.
> {.is-danger}

---

> Are you comfortable calling municipalities directly to clarify property-use or CO questions before I spend money on inspections?

> Is willing to make calls, obtain records, and help resolve basic municipal questions before you incur unnecessary costs.
> {.is-success}

> Refuses to investigate municipal issues at all or expects you to handle every question independently.
> {.is-danger}

---

> If I find a property myself, does your agreement still require me to use you or pay you?

> Gives a clear answer based on the actual buyer-agency agreement and explains any exceptions or continuing obligations.
> {.is-success}

> Gives a vague answer or says it depends without showing you exactly what the agreement requires.
> {.is-danger}

---

> Can we make the buyer-agency agreement limited by county, property, or a short trial period rather than signing a huge exclusive territory immediately?

> Is open to reasonable limits on geography, duration, or scope, especially before you've established a working relationship.
> {.is-success}

> Insists on a long-term exclusive agreement covering a very broad territory without flexibility.
> {.is-danger}

---

> How are you compensated, and under what circumstances could I owe you money directly?

> Clearly explains their fee, how seller-paid compensation or concessions affect it, and exactly when you could owe money out of pocket.
> {.is-success}

> Is vague about compensation, says you don't need to worry about it, or avoids explaining your contractual obligations.
> {.is-danger}

---

> Would you ever represent both me and the seller in the same transaction? If so, how do you handle that?

> Clearly explains the rules around dual agency or designated agency, the conflicts involved, and what consent would be required.
> {.is-success}

> Downplays the conflict or suggests there is no meaningful disadvantage to having the same agent represent both sides.
> {.is-danger}

---

> What do you expect from me as a buyer?

> Gives reasonable expectations around communication, financing readiness, availability, decision-making, and providing feedback on properties.
> {.is-success}

> Expects you to make quick offers, discourages detailed analysis, or seems focused primarily on getting you under contract as soon as possible.
> {.is-danger}
